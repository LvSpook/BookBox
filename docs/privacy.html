Privacy

Short version: your bookmarks stay in your browser. Nothing goes anywhere unless you turn on an AI key or sync. There is no server and no account.

No account No telemetry Local storage only

What BookBox stores

Everything BookBox saves stays inside your browser: your bookmarks and folders, the tags, notes and read-later marks you add, any page text you choose to index, your saved searches, rules and settings, plus a rolling set of local backups and a 30-day trash.

There is no server and no account system.

What leaves your computer

Nothing, unless you switch a feature on yourself.

AI sorting, summaries, Ask

  • 01If you paste a key from Google Gemini, Groq or OpenRouter, the bookmarks you choose to sort or ask about are sent to that provider.
  • 02BookBox sends only the titles, addresses, tags and page text needed for that request.
  • 03Smart Local sorting, local summaries and local answers never touch the network.

Dead-link checks

  • 01BookBox requests each saved address to see whether it still resolves.
  • 02Those requests go to sites you already bookmarked, with no other data attached.

Cross-device sync

  • 01If you turn it on, your preferences and tag names go to your browser vendor's sync service. For Chrome, that is Google's.
  • 02Page text, notes, history and your API key are never synced.

What BookBox never does

  • 01No analytics or telemetry.
  • 02No sending your library to the developer or anyone else.
  • 03No reading pages you have not chosen to save or index.
  • 04No selling, renting or sharing data.
  • 05No ads inside the extension.

Permissions and why they are needed

Browser permissions BookBox requests
PermissionWhy BookBox asks for it
bookmarksTo read, organise and clean up your bookmarks.
storage and unlimitedStorageTo keep your library, backups and page-text index locally.
historyTo show which saved pages you actually visit.
tabsTo open and save pages you choose.
contextMenusFor the right-click "Save this page" action.
alarmsFor an optional scheduled dead-link check.
Access to all sitesOnly to fetch page text you ask to index, and to check whether saved links still work.

Your control

You can export your library any time as HTML, JSON, Markdown or CSV. You can clear the page-text index, empty the trash, and remove all stored data by uninstalling the extension, since the data lives in your browser.
