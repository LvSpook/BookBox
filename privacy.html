Privacy

What Bookbox keeps, and what it sends.

Short version: your bookmarks stay in your browser. Nothing goes anywhere unless you turn on an AI key or sync. Updated 21 November 2026.

What Bookbox stores

Everything Bookbox saves stays inside your browser: your bookmarks and folders, the tags, notes and read-later marks you add, any page text you choose to index, your saved searches, rules and settings, plus a rolling set of local backups and a 30-day trash.

There is no server and no account system.

What leaves your computer

Nothing, unless you switch a feature on yourself.

  • AI sorting, summaries, and Ask your library. If you paste a key from Google Gemini, Groq, or OpenRouter, the bookmarks you choose to sort or ask about are sent to that provider. Bookbox sends only the titles, addresses, tags and page text needed for that request. Smart Local sorting, local summaries, and local answers never touch the network.
  • Dead-link checks. Bookbox requests each saved address to see whether it still resolves. Those requests go to sites you already bookmarked, with no other data attached.
  • Cross-device sync. If you turn it on, your preferences and tag names go to your browser vendor's sync service — Google's, for Chrome. Page text, notes, history, and your API key are never synced.

What Bookbox never does

  • No analytics or telemetry.
  • No sending your library to the developer or anyone else.
  • No reading pages you have not chosen to save or index.
  • No selling, renting, or sharing data.
  • No ads inside the extension.

Permissions and why they are needed

  • bookmarks — to read, organise and clean up your bookmarks.
  • storage, unlimitedStorage — to keep your library, backups and page-text index locally.
  • history — to show which saved pages you actually visit.
  • tabs — to open and save pages you choose.
  • contextMenus — for the right-click "Save this page" action.
  • alarms — for an optional scheduled dead-link check.
  • Access to all sites — only to fetch page text you ask to index and to check whether saved links still work.

Your control

You can export your library any time as HTML, JSON, Markdown, or CSV. You can clear the page-text index, empty the trash, and remove all stored data by uninstalling the extension, since the data lives in your browser.
